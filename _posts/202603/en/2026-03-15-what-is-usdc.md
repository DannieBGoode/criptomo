While USDT dominates by volume, USDC has established itself as the regulated alternative of reference. Created in 2018 by Circle and Coinbase, USDC offers what many investors look for in a stablecoin: verifiable transparency and solid regulatory standing, especially relevant under the EU's MiCA framework.

## What is USDC

USDC (USD Coin) is a stablecoin issued by Circle, initially in partnership with Coinbase through the Centre consortium (now managed directly by Circle). Launched in 2018 as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum, it is also available on Solana, Stellar, Algorand, Avalanche, Base, and other networks.

Like USDT, 1 USDC = $1 USD. The fundamental difference lies in how that dollar is backed.

## Reserves and transparency

Circle publishes monthly attestations by Deloitte (since 2022). USDC reserves consist exclusively of:
- Cash in insured bank accounts
- Short-term US Treasury bills (T-bills)

No commercial paper, no loans to affiliated companies. This is the key differentiator from USDT, which has faced controversy over reserve composition.

## Regulatory advantage post-MiCA

The EU's MiCA regulation, in force since 2024, sets strict requirements for stablecoin issuers: full reserves, independent audits, and authorization as an e-money token issuer.

Circle has been one of the most proactive issuers in adapting to MiCA — a significant advantage for European users, as USDC can operate with full guarantees in the EU while USDT faces greater regulatory uncertainty.

## When to choose USDC over USDT

Choose USDC if you prioritize **reserve transparency**, operate primarily in the **EU** under MiCA, or use it for **long-term savings** (see [how to protect your savings from inflation](/how-to-protect-savings-inflation-stablecoins/)).

USDT remains the better choice if you need the highest possible liquidity or primarily work on Tron (TRC-20) for its low fees.
